================================================================================
  BARBER SHOP BOOKING PLATFORM: PROJECT PLAN
  Built on the FastAPI boilerplate (Fastro) in this repository
================================================================================

  Short answer: YES. This boilerplate is a very good fit. It already gives you
  users, login, roles and permissions, an admin panel, rate limiting,
  background jobs and Docker. What we add is the barber shop "business
  layer" on top: shops, staff, services, schedules and bookings.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. THE IDEA IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  An online booking platform for barber shops with three kinds of people:

    * SHOP OWNER (admin): runs the shop. Adds and manages barbers, services,
      prices and opening hours. Sees every booking and the reports.
    * STAFF (barber): logs in to see only THEIR OWN schedule for the day and
      THEIR OWN past clients. Can't change shop settings or see other
      barbers' data.
    * CUSTOMER (user): browses services, sees only barbers who are free at
      the time they want, books a slot, and can cancel or reschedule.

  The core promise is NO DOUBLE BOOKINGS: a barber can never have two
  appointments that overlap, and a customer can never be booked in two places
  at the same time.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. ROLES AND WHAT EACH ONE CAN DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Legend:  [Y] allowed    [own] only their own data    [-] not allowed

  Action                                   Owner   Staff    Customer
  ---------------------------------------- ------- -------- ---------
  Add / edit / deactivate staff            [Y]     [-]      [-]
  Edit a staff profile (name, photo, bio)  [Y]     [-] (*)  [-]
  Manage services & prices                 [Y]     [-]      [-]
  Set shop opening hours / holidays        [Y]     [-]      [-]
  Set a barber's working hours / days off  [Y]     [-] (**) [-]
  See all bookings in the shop             [Y]     [-]      [-]
  See a barber's day schedule              [Y]     [own]    [-]
  See client history                       [Y]     [own]    [-]
  Mark booking done / no-show              [Y]     [own]    [-]
  Browse services and available barbers    [Y]     [Y]      [Y]
  Create a booking                         [Y]     [-] (***) [Y]
  Cancel / reschedule a booking            [Y]     [-]      [own]
  See their own booking history            n/a     n/a      [own]
  Reports (revenue, busiest hours, etc.)   [Y]     [-]      [-]

  (*)   As you asked, staff cannot edit the staff page. Optional later: let
        them change only their own photo and bio.
  (**)  Optional later: staff can REQUEST time off, and the owner approves.
  (***) Optional add-on: staff create walk-in bookings for customers who
        come in without an appointment (see section 10).

  How this maps onto the boilerplate:
    * The boilerplate already has roles and permission strings, for example
      "booking.read_all" and "staff.update" (modules/role/). We create three
      roles: "owner", "staff" and "customer", each with its own set of
      permissions.
    * "is_superuser" stays reserved for YOU, the platform operator, not the
      shop owner.
    * One rule that matters a lot: "staff can only see their own data" is
      NOT enforced by hiding buttons in the app. The server filters every
      query with "WHERE barber = the logged-in user". Even if a barber types
      another barber's booking ID into the URL, they get a 404, so it can't
      be bypassed.
    * Staff can't log in as each other: every staff member has their own
      account and password. The boilerplate's session login already handles
      this (one session belongs to one user, with lockout after repeated wrong
      passwords). The owner creates the staff account, and the barber sets
      their own password through an invite link.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. SAAS: ONE SHOP OR MANY SHOPS?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  You said "SaaS", which usually means MANY barber shops sign up and use the
  same platform, each paying a subscription. I recommend building it that way
  from day one, even if you start with just one shop:

    * Every business table gets a "shop_id" column.
    * An owner only ever sees data for their own shop.
    * A barber belongs to one shop.
    * A customer account can book at any shop on the platform.

  Adding shop_id later is painful. Adding it now costs almost nothing, and a
  single shop is simply "a platform with one shop in it".

  The boilerplate's "tiers" become the shop's SUBSCRIPTION PLAN, for example
  Free (1 barber), Pro (up to 5 barbers) and Business (unlimited, multiple
  branches).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. DATA MODEL (DATABASE TABLES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Already in the boilerplate: user, roles, user_roles, tiers, rate_limits.

  New tables:

  shops
    id, name, slug (used in the URL, e.g. /s/fade-factory), address, phone,
    timezone (VERY important, e.g. "Asia/Kolkata"), owner_user_id, tier_id,
    booking_rules (minimum notice, how far ahead customers can book,
    cancellation window), is_active

  shop_hours
    shop_id, weekday (0-6), open_time, close_time
    (a closed day simply has no row)

  shop_closures
    shop_id, date, reason (for holidays and special closures)

  staff
    id, shop_id, user_id (their login), display_name, photo, bio,
    is_active, is_bookable_online, joined_at

  services
    id, shop_id, name ("Haircut", "Beard trim", "Haircut + Beard"),
    duration_minutes, price, buffer_minutes (cleanup time after the
    appointment), is_active

  staff_services
    staff_id, service_id, optional custom price/duration
    (not every barber does every service, and a senior barber can charge more)

  staff_working_hours
    staff_id, weekday, start_time, end_time
    (a barber's shift, which is always inside the shop's opening hours; one
    weekday can have two rows to allow a lunch break)

  staff_time_off
    staff_id, starts_at, ends_at, reason (leave, sick day, personal errand)

  bookings            <-- the heart of the system
    id, shop_id, staff_id, customer_id, service_id,
    starts_at, ends_at (stored in UTC; ends_at = start + duration + buffer),
    status: pending | confirmed | completed | cancelled | no_show,
    price_at_booking (copied, so later price changes don't rewrite history),
    customer_note, staff_note (private to the shop),
    created_by (customer / owner / staff), cancelled_at, cancel_reason,
    created_at, updated_at

  booking_events (audit log)
    booking_id, who, what changed, when
    (useful for sorting out arguments like "I never cancelled that!")


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. NO DOUBLE BOOKING: HOW WE GUARANTEE IT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  This is the most important technical part, so here it is in detail.

  THE PROBLEM
    Two customers open the app at the same time and both see "Rahul, 5:00pm
    free". Both tap "Book" within the same second. If the app only checks
    "is it free?" and then saves, BOTH checks pass and Rahul gets two clients
    at 5pm. Checking in application code alone is NOT enough.

  THE SOLUTION: let the database refuse it (a PostgreSQL exclusion constraint)
    PostgreSQL can enforce "no two rows may have overlapping time ranges for
    the same barber" at the database level. Even if 100 requests arrive at
    once, exactly one of them wins and the rest are rejected.

      CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS btree_gist;

      ALTER TABLE bookings ADD CONSTRAINT no_barber_overlap
        EXCLUDE USING gist (
          staff_id WITH =,
          tstzrange(starts_at, ends_at, '[)') WITH &&
        )
        WHERE (status IN ('pending', 'confirmed'));

    What that means in plain English:
      * "staff_id WITH =": the rule applies to rows for the same barber.
      * "tstzrange ... WITH &&": their time ranges must not overlap.
      * "[)": 5:00-5:30 and 5:30-6:00 do NOT clash (back-to-back is fine).
      * "WHERE status ...": cancelled and completed bookings don't block the
        slot, so a cancelled slot frees up straight away.

  SAME RULE FOR THE CUSTOMER
    A second constraint stops one customer holding overlapping bookings
    (for example, booked with two barbers at the same time):

      ALTER TABLE bookings ADD CONSTRAINT no_customer_overlap
        EXCLUDE USING gist (
          customer_id WITH =,
          tstzrange(starts_at, ends_at, '[)') WITH &&
        )
        WHERE (status IN ('pending', 'confirmed'));

  WHAT THE CUSTOMER SEES
    When the database rejects the booking, the API catches the error and
    returns "409 Conflict: Sorry, that slot was just taken" along with fresh
    available slots, so the customer can pick another one in one tap.

  OTHER CHECKS (in the booking service, before saving)
    * The time is inside the shop's opening hours and not on a closure day.
    * It is inside the barber's working hours and not during their time off.
    * The barber actually offers that service.
    * It respects the minimum notice (e.g. no booking 5 minutes before) and
      the maximum horizon (e.g. up to 30 days ahead).
    * It is not in the past.
    * The customer doesn't already have too many upcoming bookings (to stop
      spam; e.g. at most 3 upcoming).
    Everything above runs inside ONE database transaction.

  TIME ZONES
    Store everything in UTC and convert using the shop's timezone for
    display. This avoids bugs around daylight-saving changes and makes
    multi-country support possible later.

 6. "SHOW ONLY AVAILABLE BARBERS": HOW AVAILABILITY WORKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  The customer's flow:
    1. Choose a shop
    2. Choose a service (e.g. Haircut, 30 min)
    3. Choose a date
    4. See the free time slots and ONLY the barbers who are free for them
       (plus an "Any available barber" option, which is popular)
    5. Confirm

  How the slots are calculated, for each barber who offers that service:

    free time = barber's working hours for that weekday
                ∩ shop opening hours
                − shop closures
                − barber's time off
                − existing pending/confirmed bookings (plus their buffers)

  Then chop the free time into start times on a grid (e.g. every 15
  minutes), keeping only starts where "start + service duration + buffer"
  still fits inside a free block.

  API:  GET /api/v1/shops/{slug}/availability?service_id=3&date=2026-10-05
  Response: a list of times, each with the barbers free at that time.

  Performance: cache the result for ~30 seconds using the boilerplate's
  @cache decorator, and clear that cache whenever a booking for the shop
  and day is created or cancelled. Section 5 still guarantees correctness
  even when the cache is slightly stale.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 7. WHAT EACH PERSON SEES (SCREENS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  SHOP OWNER (admin dashboard)
    * Today at a glance: a calendar with one column per barber.
    * Staff: add a barber (sends an invite email or SMS), edit details,
      deactivate, set working hours and time off, choose their services.
    * Services & prices.
    * Shop settings: hours, closures, booking rules, cancellation policy.
    * All bookings: search and filter; create, move or cancel any booking.
    * Customers list with visit history.
    * Reports: bookings per day, revenue, busiest hours, top barber,
      no-show rate, repeat-customer rate.

  STAFF (barber app, ideally mobile-friendly)
    * "My day": today's appointments in order, with customer name, service,
      time and any note.
    * Buttons to mark a booking Completed or No-show.
    * "My clients": history of past clients, what service they had and the
      barber's private notes (e.g. "#2 fade, likes it short on top").
    * A read-only view of their own weekly schedule.
    * NOTHING else: no other barbers' schedules, no shop settings, no prices.

  CUSTOMER
    * Sign up / log in (email and password, or Google).
    * Browse services and prices.
    * Book: service, then date, then time, then barber (only available ones
      shown).
    * My bookings: upcoming ones (cancel or reschedule within the policy)
      and past ones.
    * Booking confirmation and reminders.

 8. API ENDPOINTS (FIRST VERSION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Public / customer
    GET    /shops/{slug}                        shop info, hours
    GET    /shops/{slug}/services               services + prices
    GET    /shops/{slug}/staff                  active, bookable barbers
    GET    /shops/{slug}/availability           free slots + free barbers
    POST   /bookings                            create a booking
    GET    /me/bookings                         my upcoming and past bookings
    POST   /me/bookings/{id}/cancel             cancel (within the policy)
    POST   /me/bookings/{id}/reschedule         move to a new slot

  Staff (always filtered to the logged-in barber)
    GET    /staff/me/schedule?date=...          my day
    GET    /staff/me/clients                    my client history
    POST   /staff/me/bookings/{id}/complete
    POST   /staff/me/bookings/{id}/no-show
    PATCH  /staff/me/bookings/{id}/note         private note

  Owner (always filtered to the owner's shop)
    CRUD   /owner/staff
    CRUD   /owner/staff/{id}/working-hours
    CRUD   /owner/staff/{id}/time-off
    CRUD   /owner/services
    CRUD   /owner/shop/hours, /owner/shop/closures
    GET    /owner/bookings?date=&staff_id=&status=
    POST   /owner/bookings                      book for a customer (e.g. by phone)
    PATCH  /owner/bookings/{id}                 move or cancel
    GET    /owner/customers
    GET    /owner/reports/...

  Each group maps to a new module folder in backend/src/modules/ (shop,
  staff, service, availability, booking), following the same pattern as
  the existing modules.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 9. SECURITY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  * Every owner and staff query filters by shop_id (and by staff_id for
    staff), taken from the logged-in user, NEVER from the request body or
    URL.
  * Automated tests for "barber A tries to read barber B's booking, and
    gets 404" and "owner of shop 1 tries to read shop 2's data, and gets
    404".
  * Only owners can create staff accounts. Customers sign up on their own
    and always get the "customer" role; nobody can sign themselves up as
    staff.
  * Customer phone numbers and emails are visible only to that shop's
    owner and to the barber actually serving them.
  * Rate-limit the booking and availability endpoints (the boilerplate
    already has this) so bots can't grab every slot.
  * Keep the boilerplate's production validator switched on (strong
    secrets, locked-down CORS).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 10. SUGGESTED ADD-ONS (my recommendations)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HIGH VALUE, add early
    * Reminders: SMS or WhatsApp 24 hours and 2 hours before the
      appointment. This is the #1 way to cut no-shows. Use the
      boilerplate's Taskiq background jobs and a provider such as Twilio,
      MSG91 or the WhatsApp Business API.
    * Booking confirmation messages (email, SMS or WhatsApp).
    * Cancellation policy: e.g. free cancellation up to 2 hours before,
      after that it's marked "late cancel".
    * No-show tracking: after 2 no-shows a customer must pay a deposit to
      book.
    * Walk-ins: the barber or owner adds a walk-in customer at the counter.
      This goes through the same no-overlap rule, so walk-ins and online
      bookings never clash.
    * "Any available barber" option, which assigns the least-busy free
      barber.

  MEDIUM, second phase
    * Online payments or deposits (Stripe or Razorpay), which also reduces
      no-shows.
    * Waitlist: "notify me if a 5pm slot opens up". When someone cancels,
      the first person on the waitlist gets a message.
    * Reviews and ratings for barbers after a completed visit.
    * Customer preferences and photos of previous cuts ("same as last
      time").
    * Staff commission report (e.g. 40% of each service goes to the
      barber).
    * Break management: lunch breaks as blocked time.
    * Google Calendar sync for barbers.

  LATER, growth features
    * Multiple branches per owner.
    * Loyalty points ("10th haircut free"), gift cards, discount codes.
    * Packages and memberships ("4 haircuts a month").
    * Products and retail sales (hair wax, etc.).
    * A public shop page with a QR code to put on the shop wall.
    * A mobile app (React Native or Flutter) talking to the same API.
    * Subscription billing for shops using the platform, i.e. your SaaS
      revenue (maps onto the boilerplate's tiers).
    * A "Book with us" widget that shops embed on their own website or
      Instagram link.

 11. TECH STACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Backend      : this boilerplate (FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Redis, Taskiq)
  Frontend     : Next.js (React) or Vue. One web app with three areas
                 (/owner, /staff, /book), mobile-friendly so barbers can
                 use it on their phones.
  Database     : PostgreSQL (needed for the no-overlap constraint)
  Messaging    : Twilio / MSG91 / WhatsApp Business API, plus an email
                 provider (Resend or SES)
  Payments     : Razorpay (India) or Stripe
  Hosting      : a single VPS with Docker to start (the boilerplate's `bp`
                 CLI generates the compose file), or Railway/Render. Neon or
                 Supabase for managed Postgres.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 12. BUILD ROADMAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  PHASE 0: Setup (a few days)
    * Push the boilerplate to your GitHub, generate the first migration,
      get it running with Docker.
    * Create the owner / staff / customer roles and their permissions.

  PHASE 1: Core booking MVP (2-3 weeks)
    * Modules: shop, staff, service, working hours, time off.
    * Booking module with BOTH no-overlap constraints, plus tests that fire
      many simultaneous bookings at the same slot and prove only one wins.
    * Availability endpoint.
    * Owner can manage everything; customers can book and cancel.
    * Staff "My day" and "My clients".
    * Start with a single shop in production.

  PHASE 2: Make it reliable for a real shop (1-2 weeks)
    * Confirmation messages and reminders (Taskiq jobs).
    * Cancellation policy, no-show tracking, walk-ins.
    * Owner dashboard calendar view and basic reports.
    * Frontend polish on mobile.

  PHASE 3: Turn it into SaaS (2-4 weeks)
    * Self-service shop sign-up and onboarding wizard.
    * Subscription plans and billing per shop.
    * Public shop pages, QR code, embeddable widget.

  PHASE 4: Growth
    * Payments and deposits, waitlist, reviews, loyalty, multi-branch,
      mobile app.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 13. OPEN QUESTIONS TO DECIDE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Is this for ONE shop (yours or a client's) or a SaaS for MANY shops?
     (The plan supports both, but it changes Phase 3 and pricing.)
  2. Can a customer book several services in one visit (haircut + beard
     with the same barber, back to back)? Recommended: yes, stored as one
     booking with a total duration.
  3. Do customers need an account, or can they book as a guest with just
     a phone number and a one-time code (OTP)? Guest booking with OTP
     usually gets more bookings.
  4. Payment at the shop only, or online deposits too?
  5. Which country or countries first? This decides the SMS/WhatsApp and
     payment providers and time zones.
  6. Should barbers be able to block their own time (breaks, personal
     errands), or only the owner?
